# Dense CO2 in water and NaCl: source and reproduction notes

The companion CSV transcribes Tables A1 and A8-A10 from R. Mousavi,
A. Chapoy and R. Burgass, “CO2 solubility in aqueous solution of salts:
Experimental study and thermodynamic modelling”, *Greenhouse Gases: Science
and Technology* 14 (2024) 791-828, DOI
[`10.1002/ghg.2298`](https://doi.org/10.1002/ghg.2298). The article is CC BY
4.0. Only the source pressure, temperature, NaCl mass fraction, salt-free
aqueous CO2 mole fraction and displayed pointwise uncertainty are retained.

Table A1 contains eight pure-water measurements at 323.15 and 373.15 K.
Tables A8-A10 contain 26 measurements in 10, 15 and 22 wt% NaCl at 323.15,
373.15 and 423.15 K. Pressure is converted exactly from bar to MPa by dividing
by ten. Source mole fractions are converted to mole percent by multiplying by
100; the numerical values and displayed precision are otherwise unchanged.

This is a no-fit benchmark. The source observable excludes salt from the
aqueous denominator, so the assessment compares it with molecular CO2 over
CO2 plus water. Reported uncertainty is evidence, not an acceptance tolerance.
The same-paper pure-water measurements diagnose the dense CO2/water baseline;
the NaCl series constrains only an electroneutral NaCl-level aqueous response.
Neither individual Na+-CO2 and Cl--CO2 allocations nor mixed-salt interactions
are identifiable from these rows. The already published Table A24 mixed brine,
all hydrate endpoints, He-Morse, Yasunishi-Yoshida, Carroll and KCl Tables
A11-A13 remain holdouts and are not calibrated here.
